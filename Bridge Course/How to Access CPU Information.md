# Hardware Specifications Guide

This guide outlines how to find and analyze your computer's core hardware components on Windows.

## 🖥️ System Summary (CPU, RAM, OS)

To check your primary hardware specs, open the **Start Menu**, type `System Information`, and open the application.

*   **Operating System**: The OS version is listed as the very first item in the **System Summary**.
*   **Processor (CPU)**: Find your exact processor model in the **Processor** row.
*   **Memory (RAM)**: Scroll down to locate **Installed Physical Memory (RAM)**.

## 📐 Display Settings (Screen Resolution)

Screen resolution determines display detail and is measured in pixels (width x height).

1. Right-click any empty space on your **Desktop**.
2. Select **Display settings** from the context menu.
3. Locate your current resolution under the **Scale & layout** section.

## 📊 Performance Benchmarks & Guidance

Use these baselines to evaluate whether your current hardware meets your productivity needs:


| Component | Metric | Baseline Standard | Power User Standard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CPU Speed** | Clock Rate | **2.6 GHz** (Standard tasks) | **3.5+ GHz** (Fast performance) |
| **CPU Cores** | Core Count | **4 Cores** (Sufficient for most) | **4+ Cores** (Gaming / Video editing) |
| **RAM** | Short-term Memory | **8 GB - 16 GB** (Optimal) | **16+ GB** (Diminishing returns) |
| **GPU vRAM** | Video Memory | **2 GB** (Data visualization) | **4+ GB** (Designers / Engineers) |

> 💡 **Upgrade Tip**: If you are considering upgrading past 16 GB of RAM, you will get a better performance increase by spending that money on an upgraded CPU instead.
